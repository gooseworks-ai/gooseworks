`video make` always ends: the run's stop signal is aborted on every end, the last report has a deadline, the process ends within 30 seconds of a stop even when a part won't let go, and a run past its time limit (worked out from its parts, at most an hour) stops as one the same command carries on. `video make <id> --detach` starts the make in a process of its own that outlives the shell (one JSON line, then it returns; detached makes take turns), `video status <ids...>` says how each video is going on this computer, `video log <id>` prints the newest run log, and `--json` prints one JSON event per line ending with a JSON result. Exit codes are stable: 0 done, 1 needs a change, 3 stopped, 4 run again, 5 update the kit, 6 sign in. The line's "retry" answers are waited out inside the step's time, and the parts loader no longer compares the published parts index (the video's lock already holds every file's hash; the withdrawn list is still checked), so a lagging index can't stop a video. A finished video gets a frame contact sheet (about 12 frames and the end card, labelled by scene) next to it, `video verify <ids...>` names it with what to look for before saying the video is ready, a product whose photos' colours show in no frame gets a warning, and the final check's warnings go up with the video for its delivery summary.
